# Moonlight in the Aether: lore bible

This is the lore for a 20-40 minute game in the style of FF9. The Moonlight Witch from *Follow Me Down Witch Way* fights with Aethermoor's battle and loot rules. The game has two towns, a few wild places around each, and a small sunstone skiff that flies her between them over a painted map.

Sources and shorthand:
- **WW** is *Follow Me Down Witch Way* (`/home/user/follow-me-down-witch-way`).
- **A** is *Aethermoor: Hearth & Heirloom* (New-game, branch `claude/cool-ptolemy-uc93gg`).
- **TH** is Thareia (branch `claude/tender-babbage-4wiplk`).
- Names in `code` are A's data ids.

## 1. Title

***Moonlight in the Aether*** (the user's choice). The title hints at the ship. In Thareia's canon, the Aether is the glowing air that sunstone ships sail on. Moonlight is her magic. On a full moon the Aether catches the moonlight and glows silver, so the title describes exactly what she sees from the deck.

The logo on the title screen reads just ***Witch Way*** (the user's choice), in WW's blackletter (Jacquard 12), cream with a moonlit glow, alone in the sky above the skiff.

## 2. Premise

**Pitch:** Wickhollow's lanterns are floating away down the river. The Moonlight Witch borrows a sunstone skiff and follows them to a fen town, where a grieving ghost has been lighting the way home for a hundred years.

The game is one full-moon night. Wickhollow's lanterns have been going out, one a night; each flame lifts off its wick and drifts down the Sable. Where the light leaves, the Whispering Rot moves in: herbs go grey, wisps go sour, and shy things bite. When the rot reaches her lavender bed, the witch follows the lights into the Gloamwood and sees the moths that carry them stream away downriver, further than she can walk in a night. So she warms Mister Quill's hands, lights his cold skiff with witchfire, and flies down the valley to Bogmire, a stilt town in Aethermoor's Gloomfen where every lamp burns a borrowed violet flame. There Nettie the Swamp Witch tells her who is calling the lights: the Lantern Mother, last lamplighter of drowned Misthollow, still lighting the boardwalk for children who got home long ago. She's "kind, and wrong." The witch fights her way out to her through the fen, sits her down and makes her tea.

### Why fighting fits her

- **Nothing she fights is wicked.** Every foe is hollowed by rot, lost or asleep. Once beaten, it is itself again. A already writes defeat this way: Hodge "sits down on his stool."
- **Her weapons are her gifts: witchfire, moonlight and brews.** The athame still "cuts herbs and nothing else." In battle it gathers, like FF9's Steal.
- **A's own rules give the fights meaning.** Radiant and Blight beat each other, and whoever strikes first wins. Every fight is moonlight against rot.
- **The boss is released, not slain.** The Lantern Mother is A's most tender boss, and a lamplighter like Silas. The fight gets her to stop, and tea does the rest.
- **Dropped:** the blade (breaks the athame rule), a rival witch such as Mother Grue (a villain), and the Rot-Stag (too grim, and from A's forest).

## 3. Tone rules

WW's rule, "dark in look, kind at heart," comes first. A's "warm epic with an edge" adds the edge: the fights are real, but the stakes stay small.

**Always**
- Moonlight, candle glow, crooked graves and friendly ghosts. (WW)
- Foes are troubled, not wicked. A beaten foe sits, roots, sinks or wakes, and says its `koText` instead of falling. (A)
- She fights with witchfire, moonlight and brews, never with the blade.
- Every roll and every foe's next move are shown. (A)
- Kind ghosts, who stay after they're helped. (WW)
- Gentle mischief: her hat, the crow, the screaming mandrake. (WW)
- Towns and sky are safe. In the air there is only the map, the moon and music.
- Nothing is lost. If she loses a fight, she wakes at her last rest with everything she had. (A and WW)
- Every piece of loot has a story, told on its card. (A)

**Never**
- Gore, blood or death. Nobody dies.
- Villains. That leaves out A's Unsmith, the Tallymen and Mother Grue.
- A curse behind the story. A two-turn Hexed status in battle is fine.
- Children in danger. A's Lantern Mother leads children away; this one borrows lights.
- Money: no gold, no prices, no silver coin. (WW)
- Relics that shatter. A foe keeps whatever you don't pry loose.
- Ghosts meant to frighten, or ghosts who fade once helped. (WW)
- The moon or Wren in danger, deadlines, Vesper Keep as a destination, or a name for the witch. (WW)
- Fights in the air, skiff fuel, grinding, the Waking, Grudges or level walls.

## 4. The world

**The map.** One painted map at night shows the whole game. Wickhollow sits in the upper left, where the Gloamwood meets the Sable. The river winds down across heath and fields, getting wider and darker, into the Gloomfen in the lower right, where Bogmire stands on stilts. She walks in and around each town and flies between them. By road it's days; by skiff, a moonlit minute. The river running downhill between them is "Follow me down" drawn as a map.

**Wickhollow (WW):**
- **The town:** her cottage and garden; the square (well, chapel, Hilde's smithy, Quill's stall); the riverbank, with Quill's skiff, the Magpie, at the old jetty.
- **Its wild places, the Gloamwood:** the lantern path, out of the bottom of the square; and the Hollow, over the stone bridge and through the twisted grove (WW's own map).

**Bogmire (A):**
- **The town:** A's "Swamp Town", with stilt houses on plank streets, a moot-hall, lanterns on poles, and here a mooring mast. Mayor Gretch keeps "the mud outside." Nettie has her hut here. In A, Nettie lives in Bogmire and the Lantern Mother's story starts there, so both are back where A put them.
- **Its wild places, the fen:** the Murkway (planks over black pools and old graves); the Long Boardwalk; and Mother's Hollow, black willows around a sunken house with every window lit. Drowned Misthollow lies beyond.

**Canon choices.**
- Gloomfen names come from A.
- The moon is WW's and the only one, so Thareia's Auros stays out.
- Thareia's sunstone skiffs and Aether come in: WW's bible asks for "a storybook past with a newer world creeping in at the edges," and a skiff is that edge.
- A's Keep, Sleepers, Brands, Unsmith and Tallymen stay out.

**What's wrong, and why it's nobody's fault.** A hundred years ago the water rose over Misthollow. Its last lamplighter led the children out along the Long Boardwalk, then went back for the last one. Every child got home; she never found out, and she has lit the boardwalk every night since. Her grief turned to the Whispering Rot (A's name for blight). The rot soured her marsh-lights, and her lamp-moths flew further for light: up the Sable to Wickhollow's moonpetal-oil lanterns, the brightest for miles.

**Why the witch goes.** Silas can't keep a lantern lit, and the rot has reached her garden. In the Hollow, the moths show her the lights are going far downriver. Someone has to fly after them. There's no clock.

### The skiff

**Where she gets it.** For years Quill flew the Sable at night in a small sunstone skiff, trading down to Bogmire's market with Inkblot on the bow as lookout. That's where his curios come from. When the cold got into his fingers he couldn't stoke the brazier any more, and the skiff has sat cold at the jetty since. His price is a swap: a Warming Balm for his hands, and a bow-lamp that won't blow out, which is Silas's flame, relit with Lantern Oil. "Bring her back with the lights in her."

**Why it fits a witch.** Witches fly, but a broom won't carry a basket, a crow and a friend. Sunstones lift when they're warm, and nothing in Wickhollow burns warmer than witchfire. In her brazier the amber crystals glow with a violet heart.

**Her name** is *the Magpie*. Quill named her for the shiny things she carried home, and a magpie is Inkblot's cousin. It's painted on both bows.

**Her look** is Thareia's skiff (`thareia/art-in/airship/ship-2-refitted-skiff.webp`), modeled in 3D in code and wearing the turnaround's own paint: a plank hull banded in brass, a copper brazier amidships, five amber crystals with violet hearts, and two sun-painted sails spread like wings.

**The new idea about herbs.** Every WW region brings one. Here it is that herbs remember. The rot makes a patch forget what it is. Clean it with moonlight and witchfire and it blooms at once, whatever the moon says. Big patches get up and walk, and those become fights.

## 5. The Moonlight Witch

Same witch, no other name, same 3D model. She already knows the ghosts. Like WW's side stories, she starts with the hag stone and two moonwater.

### In the field

| Ability | Source | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Moonlight | WW | A silver circle that shows hidden herbs, runes and rot trails. Cast on a foe's back, it gives a First Strike (A's overworld rule). |
| Witchfire | WW | Lights lamps, the cauldron and the skiff's brazier. It blesses brews and burns rot off a small patch: "Witchfire burns nothing that belongs." |
| Spirit Sight / Hag-Sight | WW + A hag stone | Sees ghosts. In fog, it shows which planks will hold. |
| Gathering | WW | WW's rules, plus one new rule, **hollowed**: clean the patch before you pick. |
| Flying | TH skiff | From the Wickhollow jetty or Bogmire's mast, over the painted map. No fights in the air. |
| Rest | WW bed + A Hearthfire | Heal and save at her armchair, Silas's bench, the skiff's deck, Nettie's hut, or the lamp-post bench at the end of the Long Boardwalk, the last dry landing before Mother's Hollow. She never sleeps, so the moon stays full. |

### In battle

Her commands are A's, renamed:

| Hers | A's | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Witchfire** | Attack | d20 against Guard for 1d8 Ember, with A's grazes and Legend Strikes. |
| **Moonlight** (MP) | Skills | *Silver Circle*: 1d4 Radiant to every foe, shows each foe's next two intents, and reveals hidden foes and herbs. *Moonbeam*: 2d6 Radiant to one foe. *Bless*: one ally becomes Moonlit and Warded 1d6. |
| **Brew** | Items | Drink or throw a brew. |
| **Gather** | new (like FF9 Steal) | The athame cuts an herb off a plant foe. It never does damage. WW's gathering rules apply. |
| **Be Still** | Defend | +2 Guard, half damage, +2 MP. Named for WW's Stillness. |
| **Full Moon** | Legend Surge | The gauge is a waxing moon. When it's full, her best relic fires with A's card slam. With no relic, *Moonrise* does 2d8 Radiant to one foe and strips Hollowed from every foe. |
| **Slip Away** | Flee | A's flee roll. It never works against a boss. |

A hero at 0 HP sits down with her hat over her eyes. A Heartsease Tonic gets her up.

**The rot in battle.** Foes can carry a new A-style Omen, **Hollowed**, named for A's Hollowed Ranger. A Hollowed foe also counts as Blight, and its hits add Rotting. Radiant and Blight beat each other, and whoever strikes first wins. So if her moonlight lands before the foe acts, the Omen breaks. After that, only Moonrise or Remembrance Incense can break it. The player times it on the Initiative Ribbon.

**Aspects.** A's wheel is unchanged. Ember is witchfire, Radiant is moonlight, Blight is rot, Verdant is herbs, Tide is the river and fen, Frost is chill, Stone is graves, and Storm is wind (mostly for hats).

**Statuses.** All 22 of A's statuses keep their rules. Five get new names: Bleeding → **Snagged**, Frightened → **Spooked**, Swallowed → **Led Away**, Unmade → **Greyed** (Nettie: "Hexed folk… just go grey"), Hearthlit → **Moonlit**.

### Voice

She is warm and dry, with small jokes. She talks to plants and says sorry to mandrakes (WW). She doesn't like fighting, and she's good at it anyway.

- "I don't fight. I garden. Firmly."
- To a glowcap: "You're not wicked. You're walking toward the wrong light. Sit."
- "It cuts herbs and nothing else. I've asked it."
- On the skiff: "It's a broom with ambitions."
- In her grimoire: "Rot is grief that nobody sat with. I sat with some. It's better company than it looks."
- To the Lantern Mother: "Everyone got home. Every one. You can put the lamp down."

## 6. Companions and friends

**Inkblot** (WW, Quill's crow, "a thief with good intentions"). He was always the skiff's lookout, and all week he has carried lights home one flame at a time. He joins her in the square. In battle he is A's Pip, the relic-thief, and the fastest on the ribbon. He only says "Kraa."
- **Peck:** 1d4 pierce. It's light, so he acts often.
- **Pinch:** grip damage (A's Disarm). Takes a relic, a stolen light or an item.
- **Kraa!:** foes must target him (A's Provoke). He gets +2 Guard.
- **Fetch:** flies a brew to a friend.
- **Surge, *Every Shiny Thing* (new):** grip damage to every relic at once. It unlocks when his tail feather comes back from his nest in the Hollow.

**Nettie the Swamp Witch** (A, `nettie`, a companion A planned but never recruited). "Healer, herbalist, witch. Two of those you can buy. The third you don't cross. Mind the jars; some bite." She knows the Lantern Mother's story. She joins after a Hush Tea: every borrowed lamp on the boardwalk shines in her window. She heals, hexes, and hits with Tide, the boss's weakness.
- **Mind the Jars:** 1d6 Tide. Some jars bite (Snagged).
- **Stir the Pot:** heals the worst-hurt ally 2d8 (A's Bog-Hag move).
- **Bitterroot:** cures Poisoned and Snagged (A's poultice: "Tastes like regret").
- **Hex:** WIS save or Hexed.
- **Surge, *Undo the Knot*** (her Hexbane Shawl): every ally sheds up to three harmful statuses and heals 2d6.
- Voice: "I'm Nettie, and I don't thank people." "Moonlight. Very pretty. Down here we use a lamp and a stick."

One is warm and one is tart, and neither will admit they like each other.

**Silas, guest** (WW, the ghost lamplighter). Lantern Oil relights his pole. He gives her the Owl charm, and a flame for the skiff's bow that "no wind or wisp can blow out." In the Lantern Mother's last phase every lamp goes out but that one, and Silas steps out of it. He fights as a guest, as A's Tamsin does. He can't be targeted. Each turn he *Lights the Lamps*: every ally is Warded 1d8 and loses Spooked. The two are lamplighters a hundred years apart.

**Friends in the field:**
- **Hilde** (WW) gives the Horseshoe charm "for that hat of yours." "Ha!"
- **Quill** (WW) swaps her the skiff and keeps the swap shop. "Everything's a swap."
- **Agnes** (WW) points out the chapel moss.
- **Rosalind** (WW), at the riverbank, saw the lights go by "like little boats." She gives the Bell charm for a nightrose.
- **Mayor Gretch** (A), in Bogmire: "Wipe your boots. No, the other way. We keep the mud outside."

**The Lantern Mother** keeps A's three phases: Lamplight; the Children's Road (Mourning: Spooked and Rotting; she calls lamp-moths); and Lights Out (Snuff: every hero Exposed). Her Lantern and Veil each have a grip meter. If Inkblot pries the Veil loose, the rot stops.

Any rabble can be Hollowed; everything in the Murkway is. Left out: A's Bog-Hags, Hodge (his toll is money) and Rotgrubs.

All eleven herbs are WW's, and each already has an icon in `art/herbs/`. Both towns grow herbs, and the wild places grow more.

Tonight is a full moon, so glowcap and mandrake don't grow. The only way to get them is to Gather them from foes. Silas's garden grows out of season (WW canon).

**Moonwater** is the base of every brew and the limit on brewing. She carries two, Wickhollow's well and the lantern path share three a night (WW's rule), and Nettie's rain-butt gives three more. Thrown raw, it does 2d6 Tide.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## 9. Loot

**Tiers.** The game uses six of A's eight:

| Tier | How it drops here |
|---|---|
| Worn, Wrought, Tempered, Runed | Random drops, with A's affixes (for example, "Willowmurk Lace Gloves of the Mender") |
| Storied | Drops unidentified; the grimoire names it at the next rest |
| Heirloom | Named relics and WW charms only; never random |

Regalia and Primal are endgame tiers, so they stay out.

**Slots.** Her hat never changes. She has three hat-chain charms (they dangle on the model), a shawl, gloves, boots and a focus. Inkblot has a leg ring and a charm, and Nettie a staff and a ring. Gear shows on the 3D models. The skiff takes no gear.

**How loot fits the tone:**
- **Nobody is robbed.** Loot is what the rot made a foe carry, or what it lets go.
- **Grip & Claim is A's, with one change.** A relic nobody pries loose stays with its holder. Nothing shatters.
- **The card reveal is A's, unchanged.** Only the Chronicle's wording changes: foes are "brought round," not "felled."
- **There is no gold.** Foes drop herbs where A's dropped coins, and the shop runs on swaps.
